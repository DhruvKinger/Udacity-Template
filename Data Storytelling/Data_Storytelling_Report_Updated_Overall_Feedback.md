## Pass

### 🎉 Congratulations on passing!

Congratulations on successfully completing the Data Storytelling project! 🎉 Your work demonstrates the key skills needed to turn data into a message that a management audience can understand and use. A strong data story does more than present figures or charts: it explains what the data shows, recognizes the limitations of the evidence, and connects the findings to decisions and recommendations.

As you continue developing these skills, focus on the following areas:

- **Keep recommendations evidence-based.** Make the connection between each important finding and the management action explicit. Readers should be able to follow the reasoning from the data to the conclusion without having to guess how you reached it.
- **Continue examining bias and limitations.** Consider how collection, processing, and interpretation can influence results. Explain how sample data differs from the wider population and how those discrepancies may affect the confidence or scope of a recommendation.
- **Make data quality checks transparent.** Clearly describe the checks used to assess the dataset, what those checks revealed, and whether any issues could affect the analysis. This makes your conclusions easier to evaluate and reproduce.
- **Interpret statistics rather than only reporting them.** Use measures such as mean, median, and standard deviation to explain distributions, variation, and potential outliers. Where relevant, describe how these patterns influence the conclusions you draw.
- **Build a clear narrative across the slides.** Give each slide a clear purpose and takeaway. Arrange the analysis in a logical sequence so the audience can understand the problem, the evidence, the main insight, and the recommended next steps.
- **Strengthen the executive summary.** Highlight the most important findings and recommendations without making the audience search through every slide for the central message.
- **Use speaker notes to explain the “so what?”.** Notes should help the presenter explain what each chart or analysis reveals, why the result matters to management, and how it supports the overall recommendation.
- **Keep refining your visual communication.** Choose charts that make comparisons and discrepancies easy to see, label them clearly, and avoid adding details that distract from the main message.

Overall, this is a good foundation to build on. Keep asking whether each chart, statistic, and paragraph helps the audience understand the evidence or make a better-informed decision. With continued practice, you can make your analysis more precise, your storytelling more engaging, and your recommendations more persuasive. Keep it up! 🚀

## Fail

### 💪 Good effort — keep improving!

Thank you for the effort you have put into this project. 💪 The project has not met every required criterion yet, but that does not mean you cannot produce a strong data story. Use the rubric feedback as a practical revision plan. Work through the gaps one at a time, checking that the analysis is accurate, the limitations are acknowledged, and the final presentation communicates a clear message to management.

Use the following checklist to guide your next revision:

- **Connect bias to management decisions.** Identify the biases that may affect the analysis and explain how each one could change the interpretation or recommendation. Avoid listing a bias without explaining why it matters.
- **Review the full data pipeline.** Examine collection, processing, and insight generation separately. Name the relevant type of bias at each stage and explain how it could influence the final result.
- **Explain the limits of the sample.** State what would be expected of the wider population, describe how the sample differs, and explain why the difference matters. Use a clear comparison or visualization when it helps reveal the discrepancy.
- **Document dataset quality checks.** Describe the checks performed, including relevant checks for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent entries, and other quality issues. Explain what was found and how it affects the reliability of the analysis.
- **Complete the distribution analysis.** Review each variable and include the required mean, median, and standard deviation where appropriate. Identify possible outliers and explain their significance instead of presenting statistics without interpretation.
- **Explain the insights behind the numbers.** For each important result, write what it shows, why it matters, and how it relates to the project’s main question. Make sure the audience can understand the takeaway without having to interpret every calculation independently.
- **Show how the evidence supports the recommendations.** Include the analysis needed to justify the executive summary and proposed actions. Make the reasoning from finding to conclusion to recommendation clear and consistent.
- **Improve the structure of the data story.** Organize slides in a logical order, state the key message clearly, and give each slide a focused takeaway. Remove or simplify content that does not help the audience understand the central argument.
- **Add useful speaker notes.** Include notes that explain how to present the executive summary and interpret each slide’s output. Make sure they cover not only what the data shows, but also why the result matters to management.
- **Review the presentation as a whole.** Check that the charts, written explanations, executive summary, recommendations, and speaker notes support the same message. Correct unclear labels, unsupported conclusions, and gaps in the reasoning.

You do not need to fix everything at once. Start with the rubric points that affect the reliability of the analysis and the clarity of the recommendations, then revise the narrative and presentation. After making changes, review the slides from the perspective of someone who has not seen the dataset before: can they understand the main finding, the limitations, and the action being recommended? Test and refine the presentation until that journey is clear. Treat this as a chance to improve your analytical process as well as the final slides. Keep going — steady, focused revisions can make a real difference. 🌱
