ID: 1
CATEGORY: overview
MISSION: apollo_11
QUESTION: What was the Apollo 11 mission and what did it achieve?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: Apollo 11 was the first crewed mission to land on the Moon. It launched on
  July 16, 1969. The Lunar Module Eagle, with Neil Armstrong and Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, landed in
  the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969 ("Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed") while
  Michael Collins orbited the Moon in the Command Module. Armstrong and Aldrin walked on the
  surface ("one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind") and the crew returned safely
  to Earth.
---
ID: 2
CATEGORY: overview
MISSION: apollo_13
QUESTION: Give me an overview of the Apollo 13 mission.
EXPECTED_ANSWER: Apollo 13 was launched in April 1970 as a planned lunar landing
  mission, with astronauts James Lovell, John Swigert and Fred Haise. About two days into the
  flight an accident in the service module (an oxygen tank failure) cost the spacecraft its
  electrical power and oxygen supply, so the Moon landing was cancelled. The crew used the Lunar
  Module Aquarius as a lifeboat, swung around the Moon and returned safely to Earth in the
  Command Module Odyssey.
---
ID: 3
CATEGORY: emergency
MISSION: apollo_13
QUESTION: What problems did Apollo 13 encounter?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: Roughly 56 hours into the flight the crew reported "Houston, we've had a
  problem". An oxygen tank in the service module failed, causing a loss of oxygen and electrical
  power (fuel cell and main bus problems) and the spacecraft started venting. The lunar landing was
  abandoned, the Command Module Odyssey was powered down to save its batteries, the crew moved
  into the Lunar Module Aquarius, and they later had to deal with carbon dioxide build-up
  and cold, limited water and power on the way home.
---

CATEGORY: disaster_analysis
MISSION: challenger
QUESTION: What happened to the Space Shuttle Challenger during the STS-51L flight?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: Challenger was lost shortly after liftoff on January 28, 1986, soon after the
  "go at throttle up" call (main engines back up to about 104 percent). Flight controllers reported
  that there had obviously been "a major malfunction". Afterwards a report said that very preliminary
  searches of the ocean where the vehicle impacted had found no evidence that the crew survived. The
  transcripts give no technical cause, and a good answer says so instead of guessing one.

QUESTION: What was the last routine call between Mission Control and the Challenger crew?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: Mission Control gave the "go at throttle up" call and the commander, Dick Scobee
  (spelled "Scobie" in the transcript), responded to it. This was the last routine exchange before the
  accident, and the transcripts note that the call has often been misquoted.

EXPECTED_ANSWER: Neil A. Armstrong was the Commander, Michael Collins was the Command Module

ID: 9
CATEGORY: technical
MISSION: apollo_11
QUESTION: What were the program alarms during the Apollo 11 lunar descent?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: During the powered descent the Lunar Module guidance computer displayed
  program alarms, notably 1202 (and 1201), which signal that the computer was overloaded. The
  crew asked Houston for a reading on the 1202 alarm; Mission Control judged the alarms
  acceptable and told them to continue with the landing.
---
ID: 10
CATEGORY: technical
MISSION: apollo_13
QUESTION: How did the Apollo 13 crew deal with the carbon dioxide build-up?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: The Lunar Module, serving as a lifeboat for three men, did not have enough
  lithium hydroxide canisters to scrub the carbon dioxide, and the Command Module canisters
  were a different shape. Mission Control worked out a way to adapt the Command Module
  canisters to the Lunar Module system using materials on board (the improvised "mailbox") and
  talked the crew through building it, which brought the carbon dioxide level down.
---

ID: 13
CATEGORY: timeline
MISSION: challenger
QUESTION: What was the sequence of events on launch day for Challenger STS-51L?
EXPECTED_ANSWER: The countdown ran through a series of holds ("T minus 3 hours and holding").
  The targeted liftoff time of 9:37 a.m. Eastern was moved to a launch window opening at 10:38 a.m., and
  the countdown resumed at the T-minus 20 minute point at 11:36 a.m. on January 28, 1986. Concerns about
  ice buildup on the external tank were discussed. After liftoff came the throttle-up call and then the
  report of "a major malfunction".
